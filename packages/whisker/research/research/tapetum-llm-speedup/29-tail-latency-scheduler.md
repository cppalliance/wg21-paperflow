# 29 - Tail-Latency-Scheduler

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — longest-job-first (LJF) reordering is a cheap, quality-neutral tail trim (~1–3 min on the 3003 s cold run) but cannot materially close the 5–10 min gap without call elimination or dual-pod sharding.
**Confidence:** high

## Findings

- [CRITICAL] **Full-run papers are launched in ascending PID order, not predicted cost.** Evidence: `cli.py:983-986` (`pids = sorted(...)` for bare fleet); explicit PID runs preserve caller order (`cli.py:980-981`). Impact: expensive papers are uniformly scattered in lexicographic PID space (no size correlation with `P####R#`); a 10-call paper that sorts late extends makespan while up to 31 client slots and 15 server slots idle during its serial in-paper chain (`pdf_judge.py:650-890`, `unit_judge.py:389-410`). LJF by predicted cost is the direct fix; expected saving **~60–180 s (~2–6%)** on 3003 s (see queueing finding below), quality risk **none** (same calls, different start times).

- [CRITICAL] **Paper cost is cheaply predictable to ±1–2 calls for LJF ranking (exact call count not required).** Evidence: (a) **md size** — `backend.get_paper_md_path(pid).stat().st_size` is O(1); papers above `MAX_PAPER_MD_CHARS=500_000` trigger H2 chunking with one extra tier-1 call per chunk, serial (`constants.py:54-61`, `adjudicate.py:239-252`); only **~6 corpus papers** exceed the cap (`constants.py:59-60`, `10-evidence-verification-cost.md:16`). (b) **page count** — `_pdf_page_count` already opens staged PDF via pymupdf and reads `doc.page_count` (`cli.py:165-175`); used today only for timeout sizing (`cli.py:1218-1234`), not scheduling. Correlates with PDF text volume and page-screen flags but **179/180 PDFs saturate `MAX_UNIT_CHECKS=5`** regardless (`01-call-count-accountant.md:30-31`). (c) **Prior sidecar** — successful sidecars expose `checked_unit_ids`, page escalation counts, `chunked`/`risk_signals` (`pdf_judge.py:431-542`, `inspect_report.py:171-182`); perfect for warm reruns, **zero signal on cold fleet** (`00-baseline.md:17-18`, `_LANE_VERSION` bump). **Cheap predictor:** `predicted_calls ≈ lane_base + min(5, ⌈md_chars/500_000⌉_html_only) + 5` (376/381 papers have `risk_signals`, `01-call-count-accountant.md:30`) + `I(page_esc)` (weak without running `screen_pages`, `pdf_judge.py:636`) + `I(ideal)` (`cli.py:1124-1159`). Measured fleet: median **7**, max **10**, mean **6.11** (`01-call-count-accountant.md:8-9`, `00-baseline.md:22-23`). Impact: sort key computable in **<5 ms/paper** before any LLM; mis-ranking a 7-call vs 8-call paper costs **~20 s** tail, not a verdict change.

- [HIGH] **Queueing theory: LJF on 381 jobs / 32 client slots (16 server slots) trims tail idle, not steady-state throughput.** Model each paper as one non-preemptive job of duration `T_j ≈ n_calls × t_decode` with serial in-paper calls (`00-baseline.md:36-38`, `03-cascade-topology-auditor.md:8`). Bottleneck is **16 vLLM slots** (`00-baseline.md:66`, `cli.py:122-127`); client `c=32` overfills intentionally. **LPT (longest processing time first)** list scheduling on identical parallel machines has worst-case approximation ratio **4/3 − 1/(3m) ≈ 1.062** for **m=16** (Graham 1969; textbook **P||C_max** bound). Heavy-tailed but **bounded** job sizes (**n_calls ∈ [2, 10]**, `01-call-count-accountant.md:8-9`) limits LJF upside vs unbounded Pareto workloads. Empirical tail: last 10-min bucket **54 papers** vs peak **102** (`00-baseline.md:37-39` / throughput `00-baseline.md:37-40`) ⇒ **~47% throughput drop** in the final bucket, consistent with shrinking active set + long serial finishers, not a full pod stall. **Quantified LJF upside:** worst solo tail = one **10-call** paper at **~20 s/call** (`00-baseline.md:24-25`) ⇒ **~200 s** with 15/16 server slots idle; LJF overlaps that work with the bulk phase. Fleet-level saving **~60–180 s (2–6% of 3003 s)** when long jobs would otherwise finish last; **0 s** if expensive papers already spread (uniform cost case — `03-cascade-topology-auditor.md:16-17` argued "tens of seconds" for in-paper serialization tail alone). Quality risk: **none** (ordering only).

- [HIGH] **Determinism: scheduling order does not affect aggregated output order or per-paper verdict inputs.** Evidence: `asyncio.gather(*(_adjudicate_one(i, pid) for i, pid in enumerate(pids)))` at `cli.py:1397-1398`; explicit comment **`gather() returns results in argument (= input) order regardless of completion order`** (`cli.py:1401-1402`). Footer counts and inspect pairs iterate `records` in that fixed order (`cli.py:1404-1410`). Note: user-requested lines `cli.py:1363-1370` are per-paper logging/inspect side effects inside the worker; the determinism contract lives at **`cli.py:1397-1402`**. Sidecar files on disk complete in finish-time order, but **`report-merged` / footer / inspect** are input-order deterministic. Impact: **safe to reorder `pids` before gather** for LJF; quality-stability across runs unchanged (same cascade per paper).

- [MED] **Chunked >500K-char papers (HTML text lane) dominate per-paper tail variance; ~6 papers, up to ~5–6 extra serial tier-1 calls each.** Evidence: `MAX_PAPER_MD_CHARS=500_000` (`constants.py:61`); `chunk_markdown` + serial `for index, chunk in enumerate(chunks): await run_agent` (`adjudicate.py:239-252`); chunked papers **skip tier-2** (`adjudicate.py:287-291`). Example **P2728R11 ~2.5 MB** ⇒ **~5–6 chunks** (`constants.py:59-60`, `10-evidence-verification-cost.md:16`). Extra cost vs single-chunk HTML median: **(chunks−1) × ~20 s ≈ 80–100 s serial** per oversized paper if it runs alone at tail. Fleet: **6 × ~80 s / effective parallelism ≈ 30–50 s** amortized; **LJF saving if one chunked paper is last finisher: ~80–160 s**. PDF lane does not chunk triage (`pdf_judge.py:612-650` sends full md in monolith/units); chunk tail risk is **HTML-only**. Quality risk: **none**.

- [MED] **55 model retries add ~100 s fleet wall, spread across the run — not a reordering target.** Evidence: cold run **55 model retries** (`00-baseline.md:18`, `00-baseline.md:59-60`); priced **54 × ~30 s / 16 ≈ 101 s (~3.5%)** (`07-retry-timeout-auditor.md:8-9`). Retry triggers are backend re-issues per call (`model_backends.py:300-410`, `judge_task.py:68-74`), **not** pydantic `output_retries` in tapetum (`07-retry-timeout-auditor.md:10-11`). Timestamps show **no end-of-run retry storm** (`07-retry-timeout-auditor.md:18`). Impact: retries are **exogenous per-call delay**; LJF does not reduce retry count; **~100 s irreducible** without backend/schema fixes. Quality risk: N/A.

- [LOW] **Implementation hook: sort `pids` once after selection, before `asyncio.gather`, using a stable `(−predicted_cost, pid)` key.** Evidence: insertion point between `cli.py:990-993` (PID list finalized) and `cli.py:1397` (gather). Reuse `_pdf_page_count` (`cli.py:165-184`), md `stat().st_size`, lane from `_source_kind` (`cli.py:1121-1122`), optional prior sidecar read for warm runs (`cli.py:1175-1194` incremental path). `_DEFAULT_CONCURRENCY=32` and FIFO semaphore wake order (`cli.py:1108-1112`) already allow out-of-order completion; only the **launch order** changes. Impact: **~30 lines in cli.py**; **~1–3 min** on 3003 s cold run; **0** on warm skip-heavy runs. Quality risk: **none**.

## False-pass hypothesis

LJF starts a **chunked HTML giant** early; it monopolizes one client slot through **5–6 serial tier-1 calls** (`adjudicate.py:248-252`) while the pod is still filling, slightly **increasing** queue depth for smaller papers mid-run. Throughput accounting looks better (shorter final tail) but **mid-run p99** could rise if MoE batch composition degrades under long-prefill neighbors (`05-web.md:11-12`, `21-dual-pod-sharder.md:16-17`). Verdict quality unchanged; operator-visible latency fairness shifts, not false clears.

## False-fail hypothesis

none found — reordering does not skip calls, change caps, or alter fusion inputs.

## What would change my mind

Sidecar `LastWriteTime` analysis on the **3003 s v10 cold run** showing the final **≤16 papers** include **≥3** papers with **≥9 logged calls** finishing after the 95th percentile completion time **and** inter-arrival gap **>120 s** with `<8` concurrent sidecar writes — that would prove a **>300 s** LJF-recoverable tail (not the **~60–180 s** modeled here) and upgrade this lever to a **primary** slice of the 5–10 min plan.
