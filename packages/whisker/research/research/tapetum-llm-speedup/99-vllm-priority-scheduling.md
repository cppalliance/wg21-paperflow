# 99 - vLLM-Priority-Scheduling

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — vLLM V1 exposes a real per-request `priority` field and a `--scheduling-policy priority` server mode, but our fleet sends no priorities today, the pod almost certainly runs default FCFS, and reordering alone cannot materially shrink total decode work on a saturated 16-slot MoE judge.
**Confidence:** high

## Findings
- [HIGH] OpenAI-compatible `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/completions` accept a request-body `priority: int` (default 0; lower = handled earlier). Evidence: `vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/protocol.py:363-371`, `vllm/entrypoints/openai/completion/protocol.py:132-140`. Passed to the engine at `vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/serving.py:369` and `vllm/entrypoints/openai/completion/serving.py:213`. Impact: **0 s on the 3003 s cold run until client wiring exists**; quality risk **none** (ordering only).
- [HIGH] Scheduling policy is a server flag, not per-request: `--scheduling-policy {fcfs|priority}` maps to `SchedulerConfig.policy` (default `"fcfs"`). Evidence: `vllm/config/scheduler.py:22,99-105`, `vllm/engine/arg_utils.py:656,1447-1448,2182`, `vllm/v1/core/sched/request_queue.py:13-17,201-208`. Under FCFS the waiting queue is FIFO (`request_queue.py:75-84`) and **ignores** `Request.priority`. Impact: **0 s today** if alliance-pod runs default FCFS (likely); quality risk **none**.
- [HIGH] In `priority` mode the scheduler orders waiting requests by `(priority, arrival_time, request_id)` via `Request.__lt__` and a min-heap queue; on KV exhaustion it preempts the **highest** `(priority, arrival_time)` running request. Evidence: `vllm/v1/request.py:329-340`, `vllm/v1/core/sched/request_queue.py:131-138`, `vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:575-579,1963-1973`. Impact: a plausible fleet scheme — priority `0` for a paper's calls 2–6, priority `1` for new papers' first calls — could shave **~5–15 s queue wait** on later serial calls (32 client papers vs 16 server slots per `00-baseline.md:34-36,66`), but reordering is **zero-sum** on a saturated pod: honest fleet-wall estimate **~1–5% (~30–150 s off 3003 s)**, not the 5–10× needed; quality risk **low** (preemption recomputes KV per `docs/configuration/optimization.md:47`, not different prompts).
- [MED] V1 chunked prefill already prioritizes **decode steps over prefill steps** inside each scheduling iteration, independent of request priority. Evidence: `docs/configuration/optimization.md:53-57`, `vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py:426-435,468-498`. Impact: the "short metadata decode before giant monolith prefill" win is **largely already captured**; request priority only affects **waiting-queue admission order**, so metadata-over-monolith via priority adds **<~2% (~60 s)** at best; quality risk **none**.
- [MED] Fleet wall is compute-bound, not queue-magic-bound: ~2284 calls × ~20 s / 16 slots ≈ 2855 s closes to measured 3003 s (`00-baseline.md:22-24`). Priority does not reduce tokens decoded or calls made. Evidence: `00-baseline.md:22-24,38-39`. Impact: **hard ceiling ~5%** even with perfect ordering; **cannot** reach 5–10 min alone; quality risk **none**.
- [MED] Our judge fleet does not send `priority` today. Tapetum goes through `pipeline.model_backends.VllmThinkingBackend` → `chat.completions.create(...)` with `extra_body` for thinking only, no priority field; `judge_task.py` has no priority hook. Evidence: `packages/pipeline/src/pipeline/model_backends.py:293-316`, grep of `packages/whisker/src/whisker/tapetum_llm/judge_task.py`. Impact: **0 s until** (a) pod restart with `--scheduling-policy priority` and (b) client passes per-call priority (would require an approved pipeline change or whisker-local HTTP bypass); quality risk **none** until enabled.
- [LOW] Protocol text says non-zero `priority` raises an error when the server is not in priority mode (`chat_completion/protocol.py:369-370`), but no matching validation was found in the serving/engine path (priority is stored and forwarded regardless). Evidence: `vllm/entrypoints/openai/chat_completion/protocol.py:369-370` vs absent `raise`/`validate` for priority+policy in `vllm/v1/engine/input_processor.py:242-385`. Impact: misconfigured FCFS + client priorities would **silently no-op**, hiding rollout mistakes; quality risk **none**, operability risk only.

## False-pass hypothesis
Under `--scheduling-policy priority`, repeated KV preemption of a deprioritized long monolith prefill (`scheduler.py:575-603`) could interact badly with client retry/timeouts and leave a paper's chain incomplete while the fleet run continues, producing a **missing LLM coverage** tombstone rather than a wrong verdict. Unlikely at `--max-num-seqs 16` with similar-sized judge prefills, but not zero.

## False-fail hypothesis
Starving new papers' priority-1 first calls while favoring continuations could push marginal papers toward the **900 s paper budget** (`cli.py:138`) before the monolith/metadata chain finishes, yielding `error`/timeout tombstones on otherwise good conversions — a **false operational fail**, not a quality regression in findings.

## What would change my mind
A measured A/B on alliance-pod over the full 381-paper cold fleet: `--scheduling-policy priority` + continuation-biased client priorities, reporting **>10% cold wall reduction** (≥300 s off 3003 s) with **zero verdict-class drift** vs the v10 baseline fingerprint set.
