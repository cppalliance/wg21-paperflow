# 12 - Unit-Batching-Architect

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — multi-unit packing can cut unit-call count ~30–40% in theory, but RuVerBench/Batch-Prompting evidence predicts double-digit detection loss on our long-context, full-markdown prompts; reject as a fleet lever until a holdout A/B proves ≤2% verdict drift on SHORT adjacent pages only.
**Confidence:** medium

## Findings

- [CRITICAL] Unit checks are already the dominant call bucket and serial per paper: ~1510 calls (~66% of ~2284 fleet calls), each ~20 s at 16 slots, executed in a strict serial loop with no cross-unit batching today. Evidence: `00-baseline.md:22-24`, `unit_judge.py:389-416`. Impact: halving unit calls saves at most ~1510×20/16/2 ≈ **944 s (~31% of the 3003 s cold run)** even if every pair batches cleanly; realistic adjacent-pair rate (~40–50% of selections) yields **~400–500 s (~13–17%)**, still far short of the 5–10 min target alone. Quality risk: any missed defect on a batched page propagates to the 16/381 papers whose merged verdict already depends on unit findings (`00-baseline.md:29-30`).

- [CRITICAL] External multi-rubric/judge batching literature directly contradicts packing 2–3 fidelity rubrics per call at our context scale. Evidence: RuVerBench (arXiv 2606.29920) — batching 4–5 rubrics per call drops **every tested model by double digits** on long-context agentic tasks (`05-web.md:96-98`); Batch Prompting (arXiv 2301.08721) — N-questions-per-call saves ~5× tokens but **accuracy drops as per-item context grows** (`05-web.md:62-64`); multi-objective judge packing (arXiv 2605.26046) — Spearman rho **~−5.3%**, multi-task configs often never beat single-task (`05-web.md:59-61`). Impact: our unit check is a per-page fidelity rubric with structured verdict + up to 5 defects; packing 2–3 pages is structurally identical to the failed configs above. Estimated quality risk: **10–20%+ relative detection loss** on batched units unless proven otherwise — incompatible with “same finding quality” (`00-baseline.md:77-78`).

- [HIGH] Each unit call already sends the **full candidate markdown** plus one page’s source text; batching adds more source pages but does not remove the repeated full-document prefill that dominates input. Evidence: `_check_one_unit` user message includes entire `candidate_md` (`unit_judge.py:769-776`); baseline notes whole document prefilled ~6× per paper (`00-baseline.md:43-46`). Impact: call-count reduction is real (fewer round trips, fewer guard tags, fewer structured-output headers), but **prefill savings are marginal** (~one extra page of source vs entire candidate). Net latency per batched call may exceed half of two serial calls if output grows (see below). Wall saving skews toward **HTTP/scheduling overhead (~2–4 s/call)** not decode-bound prefill elimination.

- [HIGH] The prompt and schema are strictly single-unit; multi-unit packing requires a breaking schema and prompt rewrite plus downstream splitter changes. Evidence: `UNIT_CHECK_SYSTEM_PROMPT` instructs “THIS UNIT ONLY” and one bounded source region (`unit_judge.py:129-158`); `UnitCheck` carries one `unit_id`, one `verdict`, one `confidence`, and `defects` with a pass↔empty-defects validator (`models.py:298-320`); `verify_unit_evidence` grounds each defect quote against **one** `unit_text_map[unit_id]` (`unit_judge.py:697-712`). Required schema shape: `MultiUnitCheck` with `units: list[UnitSubCheck]` (each subcheck: `unit_id`, `verdict`, `confidence`, `reasoning`, `defects`), per-subcheck `@model_validator`, prompt blocks `SOURCE TEXT (page:N)` per unit, and `_check_one_unit` → `_check_unit_batch` returning flattened `unit_results`. `cli.py` fingerprint/schema contract (`cli.py:482-520`) must version-bump. Impact: implementable, but **no speedup until validated**; output budget risk: 3× (40-word reasoning + up to 5 defects) may exceed the 1536-token judge cap (`cli.py:1029-1034`).

- [MED] Our router does **not** emit cross-page risk signals; “signal spans adjacent pages” is a hypothetical, not current behavior. Evidence: `route_pdf_units` attaches `low_recall`, `missing_captions`, `heading_drift`, `table_presence` per page and `token_delta` to a single representative page (`source_router.py:209-263`); quota selection `_select_units_with_quotas` picks by signal-type coverage then sort order, not adjacency (`unit_judge.py:175-213`). Impact: batching opportunities are **geometrically sparse** (only when quota selects consecutive `page:N` ids); no router change makes packing the primary fix for split-table or continued-list defects across pages — the monolith (`pdf_judge.py:650`) remains the cross-page owner.

- [MED] “SHORT pages” are a plausible narrow exception window but undefined in code today. Evidence: recall screen skips pages with `< PAGE_MIN_TOKENS` (50) (`constants.py:183`, `source_router.py:211`); typical WG21 pages exceed this. A batching gate might cap combined source tokens (e.g. ≤800 tokens across 2–3 pages) to limit per-item context growth per Batch Prompting (`05-web.md:62-64`). Impact: maybe **15–25% of unit checks** qualify as SHORT pairs on a sample paper; fleet saving drops to **~150–250 s (~5–8%)** with lower quality risk than long-page triples — still needs holdout proof.

- [LOW] Batching interacts badly with fail-closed coverage semantics and quota caps. Evidence: one failed batched call currently fails one `unit_id` (`unit_judge.py:419-423`); a batch failure loses 2–3 units at once, forcing `coverage_complete=false` and verdict `review` (`unit_judge.py:460-477`); `MAX_UNIT_CHECKS=5` caps routed units (`constants.py:223`). Impact: fewer calls but **coarser failure granularity**; retries (`00-baseline.md:60-61`, 55 fleet retries) replay larger prompts.

## False-pass hypothesis

A batched call on adjacent SHORT pages where page 1 carries a `table_presence` high-severity signal and page 2 has a subtle `cell_content_wrong`: the model satisfies the prompt’s primary risk context on page 1, emits pass for page 2 without grounding a defect (RuVerBench-style rubric neglect on multi-task prompts, `05-web.md:96-98`), `verify_unit_evidence` finds no quote to refute, and the paper stays `pass` at the LLM lane while a serial single-unit call on page 2 would have surfaced `review`. This is especially likely given 70% of unit checks already return zero defects (`00-baseline.md:29`) — the marginal check is often the only one that matters.

## False-fail hypothesis

A wording-paper page pair batched together: page 1 has legitimate `:::wording-remove` markup and page 2 has normal prose. The model conflates sanctioned markup rules across units, emits a `toc_leak` or `content_omission` defect with a `source_quote` grounded on page 1’s source text but `source_unit` mis-tagged to page 2; aggregation promotes a spurious defect group and caps a clean paper at `review`. Single-unit calls isolate the conversion-contract scope that `UNIT_CHECK_SYSTEM_PROMPT` enforces per unit (`unit_judge.py:129-158`).

## What would change my mind

A labeled holdout replay (≥48 anchors from the locked holdout, `whisker/CLAUDE.md` gap #6) comparing current serial `UnitCheck` vs 2-page SHORT-only `MultiUnitCheck` on the **same** 381-paper fleet slice, reporting matched verdict/defect-group F1 ≥0.98 and zero new false-clears on the 16 verdict-changing papers (`00-baseline.md:29-30`), with wall-clock saving measured ≥10% on unit-check phase. Without that A/B, the RuVerBench double-digit regression prior dominates.
