# 138 - ragas-Executor

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — ragas's flat bounded executor is the right *scheduling primitive* for independent LLM calls at server-slot granularity, but it is not a 6× lever by itself; porting it without an explicit call-DAG and fail-closed semantics would miss the 5–10 min target or silently weaken coverage.
**Confidence:** high

## Findings

- [CRITICAL] Ragas evaluates `len(metrics) × len(dataset)` as one flat job list with no inter-job dependencies: nested loops submit every `(sample, metric)` pair to a single `Executor` (`evaluation.py:244-276`), then `await executor.aresults()` drains the pool (`evaluation.py:287`). Our tapetum cascade is the opposite shape: paper-level `asyncio.Semaphore(32)` wraps the entire adjudication (`cli.py:1112-1120`), and inside each paper calls are strictly serial (monolith → metadata/outline → page escalations `pdf_judge.py:753-763` → unit checks `unit_judge.py:389-416`). Impact: flat scheduling alone cannot cut the ~2284-call census (`00-baseline.md:22-24`); wall floor stays `2284 × ~20 s / 16 slots ≈ 2855 s`. Expect ≤5–10% wall recovery (~150–300 s off 3003 s) from slot-hole filling, not 5–10 min.

- [HIGH] Ragas bounds concurrency with one global semaphore on all coroutines: `RunConfig.max_workers` defaults to **16** (`run_config.py:54`), passed into `Executor._process_jobs` (`executor.py:119-122`), implemented as `asyncio.Semaphore(max_workers)` wrapping every task in `as_completed` (`async_utils.py:58-79`). This matches our pod `--max-num-seqs 16` (`00-baseline.md:66`) but at **call** granularity, whereas we bound at **paper** granularity (`cli.py:119-127`, `_DEFAULT_CONCURRENCY=32` overfill). Impact: a call-DAG executor capped at 16 in-flight LLM jobs aligns client pressure with server slots; reusing ragas's default-16 literally would regress the measured c=32 overfill benefit (`cli.py:125-126`, −4.2% wall). Quality risk: low if cap applies only to LLM nodes, not deterministic routing.

- [HIGH] Ragas achieves cross-metric parallelism per sample because jobs are independent; our only safely parallelizable LLM stage is post-routing unit checks and (after screen) page escalations — each currently serial loops (`unit_judge.py:389`, `pdf_judge.py:753`). A flat call-DAG with edges `monolith→metadata→{units*, pages*}` could run up to 5 unit checks concurrently per paper without changing call count (~1510 unit calls, `00-baseline.md:22`). Impact: at full fleet saturation total LLM-seconds unchanged, so wall still ~2855 s floor; benefit is reducing paper-level semaphore hold time and routing-induced slot idle (see next finding), not dividing total call-seconds. Quality risk: **medium** — MoE batch-composition variance across concurrent same-paper prompts (`00-baseline.md:96`, lever 3).

- [HIGH] Our nested paper semaphore likely causes slot idle during non-LLM work: `_adjudicate_one` holds `async with sem` for the whole chain including deterministic routing, fingerprint I/O, and evidence post-processing (`cli.py:1120-1238`), while ragas jobs are pure awaitable metric calls. When many papers finish a monolith call simultaneously and enter CPU routing before the next LLM call, fewer than 16 server slots may be active even though 32 paper slots are occupied. Impact: explains ~5% gap between measured 3003 s and theoretical 2855 s (`00-baseline.md:24-25`); flat call-DAG that releases concurrency during routing/post-process could recover ~150–450 s. Quality risk: none if routing outputs are unchanged.

- [MED] Ragas per-job exception policy is soft-default: `wrap_callable_with_index` catches any `Exception`, logs `Job[counter]`, returns `(counter, np.nan)` unless `raise_exceptions=True` (`executor.py:67-84`, default `False` at `executor.py:49`). Failed jobs do not cancel siblings; `process_futures` converts exceptions to values (`async_utils.py:117-118`). Our lane is fail-closed: unit failure appends to `failed_unit_ids` and caps coverage (`unit_judge.py:419-423`), page escalation failure raises `PdfLaneError` (`pdf_judge.py:760-763`). Impact: porting ragas's swallow-to-nan pattern would speed runs by skipping retries but violates fidelity (`00-baseline.md:79`). Quality risk: **high** if copied blindly.

- [MED] `RunConfig` bundles timeout/retry, not a separate rate limiter: defaults `timeout=180`, `max_retries=10`, `max_wait=60`, `max_workers=16`, `seed=42` (`run_config.py:51-60`). Retries use tenacity `wait_random_exponential(max=max_wait)` + `stop_after_attempt(max_retries)` (`run_config.py:87-115`, `97-115`). LLM calls wrap `agenerate_text` with `add_async_retry` at call time (`llms/base.py:113-115`). Per-job timeout is enforced in metrics via `asyncio.wait_for(..., timeout=run_config.timeout)` on submit (`evaluation.py:260`, `metrics/base.py:481-483`). "Rate limiting" is only OpenAI `RateLimitError` typed for retry (`llms/base.py:340-346`), not a token bucket. Impact: retry budget is more aggressive than our 55 retries/~100 s total (`00-baseline.md:60-61`); unrelated to 6× wall target. Quality risk: low.

- [LOW] Optional `batch_size` on `Executor` chunks the flat list into sequential batches (`executor.py:127-141`, `144-178`), each batch still respecting `max_workers`. Ragas testset transforms use the same `run_async_tasks(..., max_workers=run_config.max_workers)` pattern (`testset/transforms/engine.py:66-96`). Impact: batching is a memory/back-pressure tool, not a throughput win for our short judge outputs. Quality risk: none.

## False-pass hypothesis

Flattening the cascade and parallelizing unit checks without preserving deterministic routing order could let metadata/outline short-circuit fire before monolith-grounded signals are merged, or let concurrent same-paper unit prompts diverge under MoE variance — producing a confident `pass` on a paper that serial execution would cap at `review` (coverage semantics in `unit_judge.py:374-382`, quality-stability constraint in `00-baseline.md:77-78`).

## False-fail hypothesis

Adopting ragas's default `raise_exceptions=False` + `np.nan` per failed job would mark failed unit/page calls as absent rather than `failed_unit_ids`/lane error, shrinking apparent defect coverage and pushing borderline papers toward `review`/`fail` for the wrong reason — or conversely letting incomplete coverage look complete if aggregation treats NaN as "no defects."

## What would change my mind

A tapetum fleet trace (timestamps per LLM start/finish + `vllm:num_requests_running`) showing sustained `<12/16` running slots during the cold 3003 s run despite `--concurrency 32`, proving paper-level nesting is the dominant idle source rather than call volume — would upgrade flat call-DAG from "~5% lever" to "primary scheduling fix."
