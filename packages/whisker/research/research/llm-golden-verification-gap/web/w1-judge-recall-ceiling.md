# W1 — LLM-as-judge recall ceiling for document-conversion QA (web forage)

Date collected: 2026-07-22. Topic: realistic recall/precision of LLM judges on fine-grained factual/structural defect detection in markdown-vs-source conversion.

- JudgeBench reports GPT-4o at ~50% accuracy with a vanilla prompt and ~56% with Arena-Hard prompting on objectively labeled response pairs (knowledge, reasoning, math, coding), i.e., barely above random guessing on factual correctness discrimination. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12784 — Date: ICLR 2025 (Jan 2025). Matters: even frontier judges miss roughly half of objective factual errors on pairwise tasks far simpler than full-page golden diffing.

- JudgeBench's strongest evaluated judge (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) reaches only ~64% accuracy overall; the paper states this is the lowest top score among five judge benchmarks tested and shows a 31 pp gap between best and worst models. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12784 — Date: ICLR 2025. Matters: published ceiling for objective LLM judging is mid-60s%, not near-100%.

- JudgeBench ablation: judge accuracy closely mirrors solver accuracy; for coding, solvers consistently outperform judges, suggesting verification is harder than generation for structural/logical defects. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12784 — Date: ICLR 2025. Matters: an LLM lane asked to verify conversion output may be weaker than one asked to produce it, undermining 100% verification claims.

- JudgeBench-Pro (BiasScope) reports most models perform at or below random (~50%) on adversarial pairs; GPT-4o error rate is 74.7%, average error rate 25.9 pp higher than original JudgeBench. URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09383 — Date: Feb 2026. Matters: stress-tested judge failure modes push error rates toward coin-flip territory, relevant to wrapped-cell and secno-type subtle defects.

- "Reliability without Validity" (21 judges, ~541K judgments): raw exact-match agreement overstates chance-corrected Cohen's kappa by 33–41 pp on MT-Bench; GPT-4o shows EM 0.832 vs kappa 0.451 on MT-Bench and EM 0.667 vs kappa 0.309 on JudgeBench. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544v1 — Date: Jun 2026. Matters: high headline agreement numbers do not translate to reliable defect detection; kappa ~0.31 on JudgeBench-like tasks is far from perfect verification.

- Same paper: two production-deployed judges show test–retest reliability >0.95 while position bias >0.10 ("consistency–bias paradox"); position flip rates across prior work cited as 25–50%. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544v1 — Date: Jun 2026. Matters: a judge can look stable rerun-to-rerun while systematically missing or mis-ranking defects by position, matching whisker's >=25% verdict-flip observations.

- "The Coin Flip Judge?" (50 pairwise trials × 29 tasks): mean pairwise preference flip rate 13.6%; 28% of questions exceed 20% flip rate; one question hits 56%; 11 repeated trials needed for 95% recovery of a 50-trial reference verdict. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13685 — Date: Jun 2026. Matters: identical reruns on the same conversion unit cannot yield a deterministic pass/fail gate without multi-trial aggregation.

- Zenodo reproducibility note: even at temperature=0, LLM graders flip pass/fail on borderline items; forced greedy (top_k=1) does not eliminate flips on Claude Sonnet 4.6 (1/7 items non-reproducible); Claude Opus 4.8 deprecated temperature entirely. URL: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20581781 — Date: 2026. Matters: temperature=0 is insufficient for bit-stable golden verification; newer models remove the main reproducibility knob.

- HalluScan benchmark (Llama-3.3-70B-Versatile as LLM-as-judge): aggregate AUROC 0.55 for claim-level hallucination detection across domains; ECE 0.466 (worst-calibrated method); NLI verification reaches AUROC 0.88 vs judge's 0.55. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02443v1 — Date: May 2026. Matters: published recall/precision-class metrics for LLM judges on factual errors sit near chance-to-moderate; stronger methods are non-LLM entailment checks.

- HalluScan reports HalluScore correlation with human expert judgments r=0.41 (p<0.05); judge method underperforms structured NLI/RAV pipelines on both AUROC and calibration. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02443v1 — Date: May 2026. Matters: even composite judge scores are weakly aligned with human defect labels, arguing against 100% LLM verification of goldens.

- G-Eval (EMNLP 2023): optimized for Spearman correlation with human quality ratings on summarization/dialogue, not defect recall; reports bias toward LLM-generated texts and uses probability-weighted Likert scores, not exhaustive error enumeration. URL: https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2023.emnlp-main.153.pdf — Date: Oct 2023. Matters: the canonical LLM-judge framework measures aggregate quality alignment, not guaranteed detection of every structural/factual defect.

- DeepEval documents G-Eval as "NOT deterministic" and recommends DAGMetric for fine-grained deterministic scoring; fixed evaluation_steps improve reliability but do not eliminate nondeterminism. URL: https://deepeval.com/docs/metrics-llm-evals — Date: docs accessed 2026. Matters: tooling authors explicitly warn G-Eval-style judges cannot serve as a deterministic golden gate.

- DeltaBench (ACL 2025, long-CoT error detection): best critic GPT-4-turbo-128k achieves macro-F1 40.76%, recall 57.19%, precision 37.35%; "Any Error" detection accuracy 69.17%; Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct F1 26.73%. URL: https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.905.pdf — Date: Jul 2025. Matters: published fine-grained error-section detection recall tops out ~57% F1 ~41% on annotated reasoning chains, a ceiling proxy for step-level defect finding.

- DeltaBench: strategy-error F1 for top models ~23–36%; calculation-error F1 ~22–36%; scaling Qwen 0.5B→32B raises macro-F1 only from 3.79 to 26.73. URL: https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.905.pdf — Date: Jul 2025. Matters: subtle structural/strategy defects are harder than arithmetic slips; model scale alone does not approach full recall.

- LRBENCH + Judge-R1 (ACL 2026 Findings): state-of-the-art LLM judges "struggle to identify nuanced reasoning errors in long contexts," especially factual consistency and evidence grounding principles. URL: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2029.pdf — Date: 2026. Matters: long-document golden review (full PDF/HTML pages) is exactly the regime where judge recall is reported weakest.

- NoLiMa (long-context NIAH): GPT-4o retrieval drops from 99.3% at <1K tokens to 69.7% at 32K when literal lexical overlap is removed; 11/13 models fall below 50% of short-context baseline at 32K. URL: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/modarressi25a.html — Date: 2025. Matters: defects embedded in long converted markdown without obvious string overlap (wrapped cells, secno chrome) face needle-in-haystack miss rates of 30%+ even for GPT-4o-class models.

- "Hidden in the Haystack" (May 2025): LLM performance drops sharply when gold evidence is shorter/fine-grained; smaller needles amplify positional sensitivity across 7 SOTA models and 3 domains. URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18148 — Date: May 2025. Matters: single-character/table-cell-level defects (whisker PR #286 SF→S) are in the hardest needle class for aggregation-style judges.

- "Semantic Needles in Document Haystacks" (Apr 2026): LLM-as-judge similarity scoring shows within-document positional bias (earlier semantic differences penalized more harshly); bias intensifies 2–9× when surrounding context is topically unrelated. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18835v1 — Date: Apr 2026. Matters: judges may miss or mis-score defects depending on position and surrounding noise, matching HTML section routing pollution effects.

- OmniDocBench (CVPR 2025): evaluates PDF→markdown via edit distance, TEDS, CDM, reading-order metrics—not LLM-as-judge; GPT-4o formula CDM recall 65.5% under strict perfect-character matching; top end-to-end overall scores ~77–90% composite, not 100%. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07626v2 — Date: Dec 2024 / CVPR 2025. Matters: the leading document-conversion benchmark uses deterministic metrics; no published path to 100% structural recall via LLM judging.

- olmOCR-Bench (Allen AI, Feb 2025): 7,010 binary unit tests across 1,403 PDFs; explicitly avoids "fuzzy gold reference matching or LLM-as-judge"; best anchored system ~77.4% overall pass rate, table tests max ~93.4%. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18443v3 — Date: Feb 2025. Matters: a major OCR lab chose deterministic unit tests over LLM judges because fuzzy/LLM eval cannot certify exhaustive defect recall.

- LlamaIndex review of olmOCR-Bench (2025): notes unit tests are "too coarse" for document-level fidelity (local adjacency checks pass while global structure fails); suggests LLM-as-judge as complementary triage, not a gate. URL: https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/olmocr-bench-review-insights-and-pitfalls-on-an-ocr-benchmark — Date: 2025. Matters: even deterministic tests don't reach 100%; LLM judge proposed only as soft complement, echoing whisker's advisory-only lane.

- DocScope (May 2026): among models that answer correctly on long-document QA, only 29% achieve a complete verifiable evidence chain (page + region + fact + answer); region grounding is the weakest stage. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08888 — Date: May 2026. Matters: high answer accuracy ≠ verified defect localization; golden verification requires trajectory-level checks LLM judges fail ~71% of the time.

- RCRR-Bench (2026): measures conversion quality by whether an LLM reader can answer validated questions from converted markdown (outcome metric); top systems ~94–95% overall, not 100%; uses separate Gemini Flash judge for gold-answer authoring, not defect enumeration. URL: https://github.com/ur-ai-net/rcrr-bench — Date: 2026. Matters: published "near-top" doc-conversion QA is question-answering recovery (~5% loss), not exhaustive defect recall on golden diffs.

- LandingAI DocVQA blog (2025): claims 99.16% QA accuracy from parsed markdown, but only 18/45 errors are "genuine parsing failures"; metric is downstream QA string match, not pixel-level or structural defect enumeration. URL: https://landing.ai/blog/docvqa-benchmark — Date: 2025. Matters: near-100% claims measure QA survivability after conversion, not 100% verification that every golden-contract defect would be caught.

- RaV-IDP (Apr 2026): reconstruction-as-validation fidelity gate achieves F1 0.914 for table-quality accept/reject, but full pipeline ANLS on DocVQA is 0.4224; gate-only filtering without fallback drops ANLS to 0.1408. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23644 — Date: Apr 2026. Matters: deterministic reconstruction validation tops out ~91% F1 on table quality classification; sub-100% routing is the published state of art, not LLM exhaustive review.

- JudgeLM (2023): reports >90% agreement with GPT-4 teacher on preference pairs in open-ended chat evaluation, but task is pairwise ranking, not exhaustive defect listing on structured documents. URL: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2310.17631 — Date: Oct 2023. Matters: high agreement numbers on preference tasks do not transfer to fine-grained golden-file defect recall.

- Judge Reliability Harness (Mar 2026): open-source library to stress-test judge reliability across benchmarks; cites G-Eval and MT-Bench human-correlation claims while framing reliability testing as mandatory because LLM judges are now "core" but under-validated. URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05399 — Date: Mar 2026. Matters: 2026 ecosystem treats judge reliability as unsolved infrastructure, not a solved 100% verification layer.

- Long-form RewardBench (AAAI 2025): generative judge accuracy on error-position vs response-length shows high correlation; sequence-classifier reward models more robust to length but still far from perfect on long-form error detection. URL: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40376/44337 — Date: 2025. Matters: long converted documents inherit length-sensitive judge blind spots that depress recall on buried defects.
