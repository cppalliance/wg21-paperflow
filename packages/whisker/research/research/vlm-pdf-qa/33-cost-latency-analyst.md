# 33 - Cost-Latency-Analyst

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — serial per-page VLM over the full 189-PDF / 6,266-page corpus is a 13–22× wall-clock regression vs today's one-call text lane (~1.6 h → ~21 h on a 24 GB pod); operationally sane only with det-guided page sampling and/or a whisker per-package page-concurrency exemption, not as a blind every-page serial loop.
**Confidence:** medium

## Findings

- [CRITICAL] **Today's text-only adjudication is ~25–40 s/paper serial (one LLM call), not per-page.** Evidence: `packages/whisker/research/llm-stack/00-baseline.md:49` (200 papers, ~1.5–2 h, strictly serial); back-solved mean **~30.4 s/call** from `packages/whisker/research/llm-batching/00-baseline.md:12-14` (`2159.8 s × 3 / 213 calls`). Sidecars carry no timings: sampled `data/whisker/llm/n5034.whisker.tapetum.json` has verdict fields only (`whisker_verdict`, `axis_findings`, `tier1_model`, …), matching `packages/whisker/research/llm-batching/17-latency-decomposer.md:8`. Impact: VLM must be compared against **~30 s/paper**, not per-page text cost.

- [CRITICAL] **Serial per-page VLM projects ~13–35 GPU-hours for the full corpus.** Evidence: reproduced page census `189 PDFs, 6266 total pages, median 10.5 (≤50-page subset), max 2679` (runtime `fitz.open` this run, consistent with `research/vlm-pdf-qa/00-baseline.md:80-81`). Arithmetic at **12 s/page** (mid QA estimate, see below): `6266 × 12 s = 75,192 s = 20.9 h`. Bounds: **8 s/page → 13.9 h** (sparse/agenda), **20 s/page → 34.8 h** (dense proposal). Text-only serial baseline: `189 × 30 s = 5,670 s = 1.57 h`. Ratio: `20.9 / 1.57 ≈ 13.3×`. Single outlier `2679`-page PDF alone: `2679 × 12 s = 8.9 h`. Impact: full-corpus blind serial VLM dominates a nightly batch budget.

- [HIGH] **Per-page VLM seconds: prefill ~2–4 s + decode ~4–18 s on a 7B VLM / single 24 GB GPU (serial).** Evidence: token budget from `00-baseline.md:15` (1288 px longest dim), `05-web.md:55` (Qwen2.5-VL ~256–1280 visual tokens; 28×28 patches), prompt assumption ~1 k text tokens → **~2.5–3.5 k prefill**. Decode **300–800 tok** structured verdict (vs olmocr `max_tokens=8000` full-page YAML, `00-baseline.md:16`). Sanity anchors: olmocr dense full-OCR **28.7 s/page**, sparse **5.6 s/page** ([CNL archive](https://canemah.org/archive/document.php?id=CNL-TN-2026-049)); QA decode ~⅕ the OCR payload → dense decode `28.7 × (500/2500) ≈ 5.7 s` plus **~2–4 s** vision/prefill ≈ **8–12 s/page** typical; sparse floor **~6 s/page**. Per-paper serial: **3 pg → 18–36 s**, **8 pg → 48–96 s**, **13 pg → 78–156 s** (all exceed today's **~30 s** single-call text path for multi-page papers). Impact: per-page VLM only wins vs text on **1–2 page** papers if decode stays under ~15 s/page.

- [HIGH] **olmocr throughput claims are parallel aggregate, not serial-per-page QA.** Evidence: GitHub PR [#316](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/pull/316) reports RTX 4090 draining **10–20 pages/s** only under multi-worker queue load; serial user reports **0.33–0.67 images/s** on 24 GB ([Issue #155](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/issues/155)). Paper Table 3 ([arXiv:2502.18443](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18443v1)): L40S **906 tok/s**, **~$190 / million pages** at **20% retries**. README ([05-web.md Q2](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)): **≥12 GB VRAM**, vLLM serve. Impact: do not budget from 10–20 pages/s; use **5.6–28.7 s/page** OCR bounds scaled down for shorter QA decode.

- [HIGH] **RunPod economics: ~$7–$28 per full serial corpus run; idle 24/7 dwarfs inference if the VLM pod stays up.** Evidence: RTX 4090 24 GB **$0.34/hr** community / **$0.69/hr** secure ([RunPod RTX 4090](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-models/rtx-4090)); L40S **$0.79/hr** ([DeployBase L40S on RunPod](https://deploybase.ai/articles/l40s-on-runpod-pricing-specs-how-to-rent), consistent with olmocr paper L40S rate in `05-web.md Q2`). Corpus GPU cost: `20.9 h × $0.34 = $7.11` (4090 community) to `34.8 h × $0.79 = $27.49` (L40S conservative). `SERVICES.toml:64-74` (cited in `llm-batching/00-baseline.md:28-32`): alliance text pod **billed per uptime hour, not per token**. Dedicated VLM pod idle: **$0.34/hr × 24 = $8.16/day** vs cold-start **~2–5 min** model load per session (`05-web.md Q2` vLLM serve). Impact: spin-down between batch runs; colocate VLM only when QA lane runs.

- [MED] **Hidden costs are minor vs inference except retry amplification.** Evidence: PyMuPDF raster at 1288 px longest dim measured **33.5 ms/page** mean (10 pages, runtime this run; dependency already in `00-baseline.md:75`). Corpus raster: `6266 × 0.034 s = 214 s ≈ 3.6 min` (**0.3%** of 20.9 h). PNG mean **106.5 KB/page**, base64 **~142 KB/page** → corpus upload **~890 MB** (`6266 × 142 KB`); at 100 Mb/s ≈ **71 s** wire time. olmocr retry rate **12–20%** (`05-web.md Q2` Issue #424; paper Table 6 via `05-web.md Q2`) → effective pages `6266 × 1.15 ≈ 7206`, wall **+15% → ~24.0 h**. Impact: optimize inference and sampling, not rasterizer choice.

- [MED] **Cheaper configurations ranked (savings vs risks).** (1) **Det-guided page sampling** — run VLM only on whisker-flagged pages (`lossy-table`, region drift, etc.; signals already in `_custom_select`, `adjudicate.py:193-199`). If **20%** of pages flagged: `1253 × 12 s = 4.2 h` (**−80%** wall); risk: token-preserving semantic corruption on visually clean pages (the lane's false-pass class, `whisker/CLAUDE.md` tapetum section). (2) **Two-tier resolution** (RaV-IDP fallback, [05-web.md RaV-IDP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23644)): low-res (~672 px, ~¼ pixels → ~¼ visual tokens per `05-web.md:55`) on all pages, high-res (1288 px) re-check on low-confidence; saves **~30–40%** tokens on clean pages; risk: missed subscripts/tiny fonts on tier-1. (3) **Multi-page per request** (multiple `image_url` parts, [05-web.md Q3 vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/multimodal_inputs/)): amortizes HTTP/prefill (**~15–25%**); risk: breaks strict serial determinism and couples page failures. (4) **Paper-level `--concurrency N`** already exists (`cli.py:49-56`, default 8); for per-page VLM, extend to **page-level concurrency inside a paper** via the same per-package hook — saves up to **N×** on multi-page papers; risk: MoE/batch-interference variance (`MODELS.md:69`, `05-web.md` batch invariance). (5) **Cap decode** (`max_tokens` 800 vs olmocr 8000, `00-baseline.md:16`): already baked into QA estimates; risk: truncated verdicts trigger D10 retries.

- [CRITICAL] **Serial every-page VLM for the full corpus needs the parallel-lane exemption, not default D11 serial.** Evidence: `CLAUDE.md:88` (D11): "`dissect` runs with at most one in-flight LLM request at a time. The framework defaults to serial via `_parallel_semaphore` and `_task_semaphore` at `asyncio.Semaphore(1)`. **New packages that opt into concurrent execution must do so through a per-package mechanism that leaves dissect on the serial path.**" Tapetum already uses this for paper-level fan-out (`cli.py:49-56`); a per-page VLM loop inside `_custom_triage` is **`adjudicate.py:206-207`** serial today ("D11: one in-flight request at a time"). Impact: ship **det-sampled pages + page-level `gather_concurrent` with concurrency cap**, or accept **~21 h** serial wall; do not flip global semaphores.

## False-pass hypothesis

Det-guided sampling that skips visually clean pages lets a table-cell swap or math glyph corruption survive on an unflagged page: the text lane already passed on token coverage, and the VLM never sees the page image.

## False-fail hypothesis

Serial per-page VLM on a dense 13-page proposal with heavy tables burns **~2–5 min** and returns `review` on sanctioned `<!-- tomd:lossy-table -->` markers or minor reflow, while today's single **~30 s** text call returns `pass` — operators abandon the lane as "too slow for noise."

## What would change my mind

A measured 50-page serial benchmark on alliance-pod or a RunPod RTX 4090 running `olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8` with our actual QA prompt (300–800 tok `Adjudication` schema, not full YAML OCR), logging per-page prefill/decode ms: if P50 **≤ 5 s/page** and P90 **≤ 10 s/page**, full-corpus serial drops below **~9 h** and moves from "blocked" to "overnight batch acceptable."
