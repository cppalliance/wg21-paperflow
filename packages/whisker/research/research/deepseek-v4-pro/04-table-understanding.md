# 04 - Table-Understanding

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — V4-Pro likely inherits adequate pipe-table literacy from the DeepSeek lineage, but no V4-specific table benchmark exists and published evals show persistent weakness on merged cells, transposed layouts, wide tables, and nested headers, which are exactly the WG21 conversion failure modes tapetum's `tables` axis must catch.
**Confidence:** medium

## Findings

- [HIGH] DeepSeek-V4-Pro has zero published table-understanding benchmarks. Evidence: arXiv:2606.19348 (DeepSeek-V4 technical report) and the HuggingFace model card (`deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro`) report SWE-bench, MRCR, CorpusQA, HLE, LiveCodeBench, etc., but no TableBench, TableEval, CompTab, CoTabBench, RealHiTBench, or WikiTableQuestions scores. Impact: we are extrapolating table capability from prior-generation DeepSeek models and general reasoning scores, not measuring the served model directly.

- [HIGH] The DeepSeek lineage is competitive but not frontier on table *structure* understanding. Evidence: TableEval (arXiv:2506.03949) reports DeepSeek-R1 at 72.05% on the "Table Structure Understanding" task (best open-source model in that suite); DeepSeek-V3 at 48.89%. The same paper reports 10–15% performance drops on nested and hierarchical tables vs flat tables across all models. Impact: WG21 papers routinely carry multi-row headers (feature-test matrices, straw-poll tables). V4-Pro's unmeasured table-structure skill is the core risk for the `tables` fidelity axis.

- [HIGH] Irregular-table categories that mirror tomd failure modes defeat even strong DeepSeek models. Evidence: CompTab (ACL 2026, arXiv) evaluates merged cells, transposed tables, missing values, and multi-hop reasoning. DeepSeek-V3 averages 37.42% EM; DeepSeek-R1 averages 33.62%. Per-category highlights for DeepSeek-V3: Transposed 25.00%, Merged 37.80%, Missing 44.49%. CompTab notes DeepSeek models exhibit the largest direct-prompt vs Python-agent divergence and degrade sharply on Missing and Transposed tables under structural noise. Impact: tapetum must judge whether a pipe table faithfully denormalized merged cells or scrambled column association; CompTab shows even R1/V3 frequently fail on exactly these irregularities.

- [MED] TableBench (886 complex TableQA cases, AAAI 2025) provides a lineage proxy but no V4 entry. Evidence: TableBench leaderboard (tablebench.github.io, Jan 2025): DeepSeek-R1 + direct prompt = 56.31% overall (rank ~10); DeepSeek-Chat-V3 + textual CoT = 50.56%. Visualization and numerical-reasoning subcategories remain weak (R1 visualization ~20%, numerical ~35%). Impact: V4-Pro's strong LiveCodeBench (93.5%, per 00-baseline) does not transfer to table QA; TableBench shows even R1 is mid-pack and far below human performance (85.91%).

- [MED] CoTabBench (2,700+ weakly-structured real-world tables) shows DeepSeek-R1 excels at structural counting but struggles on heterogeneous TableQA. Evidence: CoTabBench (OpenReview wcInjlUp8V): DeepSeek-V3 overall 54.4%; DeepSeek-R1 overall 69.3%. Row/column counting (easy/hard avg): R1 84.6% / 58.2%. TableQA (easy/hard avg): R1 64.8% / 52.4%. Hallucination detection: R1 65.0%. SOTA general models (GPT-4.1, Qwen-Plus) score only 53–55% overall, and CoTabLLM-7B (table-specialized fine-tune) beats them at 57.3%. Impact: V4-Pro can likely count rows/columns and detect obvious structural hallucinations, but judging semantic fidelity of cell contents in heterogeneous WG21 tables remains a coin-flip on hard subsets.

- [MED] Hierarchical and wide tables degrade all models; reasoning mode helps but does not solve layout comprehension. Evidence: RealHiTBench (ACL 2025 Findings): DeepSeek-R1 achieves the best text-mode EM (~70.91) among evaluated models on realistic hierarchical tables, yet the authors note EM for almost all models stays below 70 and performance declines as table token count and complexity rise. TableEval Figure 4 confirms 10–15% drops on nested/hierarchical structures. Impact: tapetum's prompt explicitly flags wide tables (>6–8 columns) and column-association breaks (`tapetum_llm.md` lines 68–79); benchmark literature confirms these are structurally hard, not edge cases.

- [HIGH] The 94% hallucination rate and schema-in-prompt serving path threaten table-specific evidence grounding. Evidence: 00-baseline §5.1 (AA-Omniscience via jacksunwei.me): V4-Pro hallucination rate 94%, driven by near-zero abstention. VllmThinkingBackend uses schema-in-prompt + raw JSON extraction (00-baseline §2, ~40% tool-call fallback in agent runs). Impact: on the `tables` axis the model must quote verbatim cell content as `evidence_spans`; invented pipe-table fragments that look plausible will fail `ground_spans` (00-baseline §3) and demote to review, but a confident wrong table verdict still wastes the advisory call and may mislead human reviewers reading `--inspect` output.

- [LOW] Markdown pipe syntax itself is a known lossy representation; models and converters share the same blind spot. Evidence: markitdown PR #2111 (2026): "Markdown pipe tables cannot represent rowspan, colspan, or nested table structure." crawl4ai issue #1327: rowspan/colspan HTML produces malformed markdown with misaligned pipes. LlamaParse docs: markdown has no native merge syntax; merged headers appear as repeated or misaligned cells. Impact: tapetum's own rules (`tapetum_llm.md`) treat faithful merge flattening as at most `review`; V4-Pro must distinguish readable denormalization from garbled denormalization without HTML/PDF ground truth, a task no benchmark shows is reliably solved.

## False-pass hypothesis

A straw-poll pipe table with six columns (SF/F/N/A/SA/abstain/total) where one data row has all vote counts shifted one column right: every cell text still appears somewhere in the markdown, `unigram_coverage` stays high, and whisker's deterministic gate is blind to positional swaps. V4-Pro reads the table as a 1D token stream, sees plausible integers under pipe delimiters, and adjudicates `pass` on the `tables` axis because no header label looks corrupted, missing the fact that "SA" counts now sit under "N/A". CompTab's transposed-table category (DeepSeek-V3: 25% EM) and tapetum's explicit "row or cell swap" rule suggest this is a realistic miss, especially without source PDF for comparison.

## False-fail hypothesis

A feature-test matrix where PDF colspan/rowspan was correctly flattened by repeating `__cpp_lib_*` macro names across spanned rows (faithful lossy conversion per tapetum rules: "merges flattened, but readable" → at most `review`). V4-Pro flags `fail`/`major` on `tables`, citing "merged cells lost" and quoting a repeated macro cell as evidence of garbled denormalization, when the repetition is the sanctioned pipe-table representation. CoTabBench's row/column counting strength (R1 84.6%) may make the model over-index on structural irregularity without recognizing that repetition-is-correct for pipe tables.

## What would change my mind

DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max evaluated on CompTab (merged + transposed categories) and TableEval (Table Structure Understanding) scoring within 5 points of DeepSeek-R1 on each, published in an independent (non-DeepSeek) eval, OR an internal tapetum_llm `--inspect` run on ≥20 WG21 papers with human-adjudicated `tables`-axis labels showing ≥90% precision and ≥85% recall (including swap detection on wide straw-poll tables).
