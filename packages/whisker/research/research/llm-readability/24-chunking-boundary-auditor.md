# 24 - Chunking-Boundary-Auditor

**Verdict:** usable-with-conditions — byte-level chunking is lossless and H2-boundary packing matches the tomd contract, but `_hard_split` breaks pipe tables and fenced blocks without table/fence awareness, per-chunk aggregation has no cross-chunk reconciliation, and seam defects can evade both adjacent chunks' axis judgments.
**Confidence:** high

## Findings

- [CRITICAL] `_hard_split` splits on line boundaries only; it is not fence-aware or table-aware, so an H2 section larger than `MAX_PAPER_MD_CHARS` can cut mid-pipe-table and mid-fenced-code content. Evidence: `chunking.py:76-78` routes oversized sections to `_hard_split`; `_hard_split` iterates lines with no fence/table state (`chunking.py:125-148`); contrast MDKeyChunker, which treats headers, fenced code, and pipe tables as atomic units (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23533, `05-web.md` Q5). Impact: the ~6 corpus papers above 500K chars (`constants.py:42-43`, baseline ~200 converted papers) and any future monolithic H2 section (code dump, wide table, embedded base64) deliver structurally broken fragments to the judge; the model may pass locally coherent shards while the full document is unreadable at the seam.

- [HIGH] H2 section splitting is fence-aware only for *heading detection*, not for chunk boundaries: `##` inside a fence is correctly ignored (`chunking.py:107-115`), but once a section exceeds the char budget, fence integrity is abandoned. Evidence: `_split_sections` tracks `in_fence` for H2 lines only (`chunking.py:95-122`); `_hard_split` has no fence logic (`chunking.py:125-148`); `test_tilde_fence_not_split` (`test_tapetum_llm.py:860-873`) asserts lossless rejoin and presence of `~~~\n`, not that opener/closer stay in the same chunk. Impact: contradicts the prompt rule "never reflowed or wrapped inside the fence" (`test_tapetum_llm.py:802-803`): a hard-split chunk can end inside a fence without a closing delimiter, inviting false table/code passes on partial syntax.

- [HIGH] Cross-chunk seam corruption can evade aggregation: `aggregate_adjudications` folds the *worst per-axis finding* across chunks (`chunking.py:178-184`), not a holistic re-read. If chunk A and chunk B each return `tables=pass` while a row swap, dropped row, or duplicated line sits exactly on the packing boundary between them, the folded verdict stays `pass` on that axis. Evidence: no cross-chunk pass exists in tapetum (contrast assay `_cross_chunk_decide`, `assay/pipeline.py:688-699`); decide grounds evidence against full `state.paper_md` (`adjudicate.py:221`) but axis verdicts come from per-chunk adjudications unchanged. Impact: seam-local conversion defects are the exact false-pass class the advisory lane targets; worst-axis fold is necessary but insufficient for boundary-local semantic corruption.

- [HIGH] `partial=True` blocks a clean pass but does not block a false pass on non-seam axes and is not persisted on the sidecar. Evidence: `chunking.py:76-78` sets `partial` when any section hard-splits; `adjudicate.py:245-246` demotes `pass` → `review` when `state.partial`; `TapetumResult.to_dict()` has no `partial`/`chunked` field (`models.py:106-124`, cited in whisker research). Impact: baseline documents "marked partial and can never pass clean" (`00-baseline.md:148`, `whisker/CLAUDE.md:390-394`) — the demotion path exists, but consumers cannot distinguish partial-read review from full-read review, and axes outside the broken section can still aggregate to `pass` on other findings.

- [MED] Chunk-boundary test coverage verifies lossless concat and H2 packing, not seam mis-judgment. Evidence: `test_h2_split_lossless_and_bounded` (`test_tapetum_llm.py:621-631`), `test_oversize_section_hard_split_sets_partial` (`633-640`), `test_fence_aware_split` on `_split_sections` only (`648-653`), `test_roundtrip_mixed_doc` with a small pipe table under char budget (`894-908`); no test for hard-split mid-table, cross-chunk table swap, or `partial` demotion in `_custom_decide`. Impact: regressions in seam integrity would stay green in CI.

- [MED] Char budget assumes ~4 chars/token while code-heavy WG21 windows measure ~2.5 (`MODELS.md:111`); `MAX_PAPER_MD_CHARS = 500_000` with `chars_per_token = 4.0` on alliance-pod (`constants.py:44`, `SERVICES.toml:69-70`) sizes chunks at ~125K nominal tokens, but a 500K-char code- or base64-heavy chunk can approach or exceed the 393,216-token configured window. Evidence: P2728R11 golden fixture is 2,540,150 chars (`packages/tomd/tests/fixtures/golden/p2728r11.golden.md`, workspace measurement); sighting run lists P2728R11/R12 among JSON-failure oversize papers with 7-chunk triage (`tapetum-sighting-run-2026-07-01.md:46-56`); `constants.py:42-43` notes ~6 papers exceed the char threshold. Impact: token overflow presents as truncation/JSON failure, not an honest partial-read flag — a different failure class from char-budget chunking but same seam-integrity mandate.

- [LOW] Downstream pipelines use different chunk semantics; tapetum H2 chunking is not shared with assay or `read_paper`. Evidence: assay `chunk_paper` splits on heading tree + bold subsections with coalescing (`assay/chunker.py:44-85`), sized via `tokens_to_chars(chunk_tokens)` (`assay/pipeline.py:524-527`); `make_read_paper_tool` caps at 500 lines per call (`pipeline/tools.py:70,88`); agora loads full `paper_md` without tapetum-style splitting (`agora/pipeline.py:149`); dissect package absent at this SHA. Impact: LLM-readability QA at chunk seams in tapetum does not transfer to assay/agora boundary behavior; HiChunk validates heading-aware chunking in RAG (`05-web.md` Q5) but those consumers are not unified.

## False-pass hypothesis

A 520K-char H2 "Implementation" section contains a 200-row pipe table and a long ` ```cpp ` block. `chunk_markdown` hard-splits on line boundaries (`chunking.py:125-148`), placing rows 1–120 in chunk 2 and rows 121–200 in chunk 3 with the fence opener in chunk 2 and closer in chunk 3. Each chunk's triage call returns `tables=pass`, `code=pass` (locally plausible fragments). `aggregate_adjudications` folds to overall `pass` on those axes (`chunking.py:178-184`); `partial=True` demotes only if the *overall* fold is `pass` (`adjudicate.py:245-246`), but a confident `review` from another axis or high confidence on `pass` axes still reads as "mostly clean" in the inspect report. The swapped rows exactly at the chunk-2/3 line boundary never appear in a single judge context.

## False-fail hypothesis

A paper just over 500K chars with well-formed H2 sections packs into 2 chunks with no hard-split (`partial=False`). Chunk 1's model flags a cosmetic `structure=fail/minor` (heading-level jump at a chunk boundary artifact in the prompt). `aggregate_adjudications` promotes that to overall `review` via `worst_axis_verdict` (`chunking.py:34-51`, `178-184`). The paper is fully readable and losslessly chunked, but the advisory lane recommends human review for a boundary artifact — acceptable for advisory, but a false alarm relative to seam integrity.

## What would change my mind

A labeled holdout of ≥10 oversize WG21 papers where (a) injected seam defects (table row swap, fence truncation, duplicated H2) are placed exactly on H2-pack and hard-split boundaries, (b) tapetum_llm triage+aggregate is run before/after MDKeyChunker-style atomic splitting, and (c) recall of seam defects improves from the current per-chunk fold without increasing full-document false-pass rate — would flip the verdict to **usable** for chunk-boundary QA.
